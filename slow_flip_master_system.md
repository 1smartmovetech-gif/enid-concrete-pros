# Slow Flip Master System

## Purpose
This is the operator manual for running a repeatable slow flip business.

Goal:
- take any lead or address
- screen it fast
- underwrite it consistently
- choose the right exit
- use the right document stack
- close cleanly
- resell safely
- service the note or contract correctly

This is a business system, not a hype document.
It is designed to reduce mistakes, standardize decisions, and make it possible to hand a property from intake through closing and resale with minimal guesswork.

## Core Business Model
A slow flip business works when you:
- acquire at a meaningful discount
- keep total basis under control
- choose areas with real end-buyer demand
- resell at terms the local market can actually support
- collect enough down payment to reduce risk
- create monthly spread and long-tail note profit
- maintain strong paper and servicing discipline

Core principle:
**Do not just buy cheap property. Buy property you would be okay getting back.**

## Main Workflow
1. Lead intake
2. Fast screen
3. Deep underwriting
4. Offer strategy
5. Contract and due diligence
6. Close and record
7. Prepare disposition
8. Sell on terms or cash
9. Set up servicing
10. Handle collections, defaults, and portfolio review

## Phase 1: Lead Intake
Capture these fields for every lead:
- address
- APN / parcel number
- county and state
- property type
- lot size / square footage
- beds / baths if applicable
- source of lead
- seller or source contact info
- asking price
- occupancy status
- photos source
- short condition summary
- known tax status
- known lien or title issues
- seller motivation
- timeline to sell
- whether seller financing is available from current owner

Minimum rule:
No lead enters the pipeline without an address or parcel number, source, asking price or target price range, and contact method.

## Phase 2: Fast Screen
Purpose: kill weak leads in under 10 minutes.

### A. Location screen
Answer:
- Is there visible demand in this area?
- Is this an area where working-class owner-finance buyers would buy?
- Is it stable enough to hold if buyer defaults?
- Are nearby properties occupied and maintained enough to support resale?

### B. Asset screen
Answer:
- Is this a real house, lot, mobile-home lot, or another asset type?
- Does it appear livable, near-livable, or major rehab?
- Are there obvious fatal defects?
- Is access clear?
- Is the structure intact enough to justify further work?

### C. Number screen
Estimate:
- asking price
- rough as-is value
- rough resale value
- rough repairs / cleanup
- rough taxes and insurance
- rough owner-finance exit payment
- rough down payment potential

### D. Fast screen decision
Classify as:
- pursue now
- make low offer and follow up
- hold for later review
- pass

## Phase 3: Deep Underwriting
Run this only after a lead passes the fast screen.

### A. Title and ownership
Verify:
- current owner of record
- vesting
- deed chain quality
- mortgages and liens
- tax delinquency
- HOA issues
- judgment or probate issues
- legal description
- whether title looks insurable

### B. Physical due diligence
Verify:
- occupancy
- actual condition
- utilities status
- roof condition
- HVAC, electric, plumbing
- access and ingress/egress
- neighborhood feel
- code enforcement issues if known
- safety concerns

### C. Market due diligence
Verify:
- sold comps
- active comps
- rent comps
- time on market
- buyer demand for low-down-payment homes or land
- cash investor demand as backup exit

### D. Exit test
For slow flips, answer:
- Can this realistically be resold on terms?
- What down payment is realistic?
- What monthly payment is realistic?
- What term length is realistic?
- Does the note still make sense after taxes, insurance, servicing, and default risk?
- If a buyer defaults in 6 or 12 months, is the deal still acceptable?

## Phase 4: Offer Strategy
Every deal should have at least two offer thoughts:
- ideal buy price
- maximum safe buy price

### Offer types
1. Cash offer
2. Seller-finance purchase offer
3. Subject-to style concept where legally and practically appropriate
4. Wholesale / assignment style control offer if exit requires that structure

### Offer rules
- base offer on verified margin, not hope
- include holding, cleanup, taxes, insurance, and transaction friction
- if title or condition risk is unknown, reduce offer
- if you do not know your exit, do not force a deal

## Phase 5: Closing and Due Diligence Gate
Before closing, all of these must be answered:
- Is title acceptable?
- Is funding confirmed?
- Is insurance obtainable?
- Are taxes understood?
- Is the final doc stack selected?
- Is exit strategy confirmed?
- Is there a backup exit?
- Is the deal folder complete enough to close?

No close should happen with unresolved title confusion, vague funding, or unknown insurance.

## Phase 6: Disposition
### Basic resale routes
- cash resale
- assignment to investor
- owner-finance / slow flip resale
- hybrid disposition depending on market

### Slow flip resale rules
- collect meaningful down payment
- set realistic payment
- disclose clearly
- verify buyer identity and ability to pay
- document taxes, insurance, maintenance, default, and payoff procedures
- use a servicing process, not loose manual collection when possible

## Phase 7: Servicing
Every slow flip needs a servicing policy.

Track:
- payment due dates
- payment receipts
- late notices
- insurance status
- tax status
- borrower communication log
- payoff balance requests
- default actions and cure deadlines

## Phase 8: Portfolio Review
Review monthly:
- notes current vs late
- taxes and insurance verified or escrowed
- default trends
- down payment recovery relative to basis
- properties at risk of coming back
- legal or compliance issues by state

## Decision Rules
### Buy if
- the discount is real
- total basis leaves room for profit and error
- title can be cleared or insured
- end-buyer demand is real
- the exit payment is supportable
- you would accept the property back in a default

### Pass if
- deal only works with perfect assumptions
- title is likely unfixable or too messy for the margin
- area has weak demand
- repairs are too unknown
- taxes/liens consume the spread
- you would hate owning the property again

## Standard Metrics to Track
- lead source
- ask price
- contracted price
- total all-in basis
- target resale price
- target down payment
- target amount financed
- target monthly payment
- projected monthly net
- projected total note profit
- days to contract
- days to close
- days to resale
- actual down payment collected
- payment performance
- default rate
- cash recovered before default
- realized profit

## Operating Principle
The business wins by making repeatable good decisions, not by improvising every deal from scratch.
