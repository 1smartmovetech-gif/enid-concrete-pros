# Slow Flip Business Playbook

## Purpose
This document is a working how-to for finding land and property deals, evaluating them, and building a repeatable slow flip business.

It is meant to grow over time.
As new video transcripts, notes, and examples come in, fold the best ideas into this playbook.

---

## What a Slow Flip Business Is
A slow flip business usually means:
- buying property at a meaningful discount
- controlling risk by buying right
- improving value through paperwork, access, cleanup, marketing, or terms
- selling for cash or owner financing
- creating spread between acquisition cost and resale value
- repeating the process with systems instead of treating each deal like a one-off hustle

Core idea:
- **You make money when you buy right, structure right, and market right.**

## Phase 1: Define Your Buy Box
Before hunting deals, define exactly what you want.

### Market criteria
- states / counties / zip codes
- urban, suburban, rural, or infill
- population trend
- job growth
- path of development
- days on market
- median price range
- demand from end buyers

### Asset criteria
- vacant land
- houses needing cosmetic work
- mobile-home-friendly lots
- infill lots
- acreage
- rural recreational land
- property with owner-finance buyer demand

### Deal criteria
- target discount percentage
- minimum dollar spread
- minimum monthly cash flow if seller-financed
- max all-in exposure per deal
- clean title only, or title-curable allowed
- access required or not
- utilities required or not

### Simple buy-box example
- vacant land only
- 0.25 to 10 acres
- counties with active land sales
- buy at 25% to 50% of realistic resale value
- prefer direct-to-seller deals
- exit via owner financing whenever possible

## Phase 2: Where to Look for Deals

### 1. Direct-to-seller lists
Good hunting ground:
- tax delinquent owners
- absentee owners
- inherited / probate situations
- free and clear owners
- long-time owners
- out-of-state owners
- vacant property owners
- code violation owners
- owners with stale listings that expired

### 2. Public records
Look at:
- county assessor
- GIS maps
- tax records
- deed history
- delinquent tax lists
- probate filings
- foreclosure filings
- code enforcement records

### 3. MLS and listed-property opportunities
Look for:
- stale listings
- price-reduced listings
- mis-marketed listings
- poor photos
- land listed in the wrong category
- properties with confusing descriptions
- listings ignored because they need effort

### 4. Online marketplaces
Examples:
- Facebook Marketplace
- Craigslist
- land-specific marketplaces
- local investor groups
- county auction sites
- niche owner-finance websites

### 5. Local relationship channels
- real estate agents who understand land or distressed property
- probate attorneys
- wholesalers
- surveyors
- title companies
- code enforcement contacts
- contractors and cleanout crews
- local bird dogs

### 6. Driving / virtual driving for dollars
Look for:
- neglected lots
- boarded houses
- overgrown parcels
- dumped-on land
- partially improved lots
- signs of abandonment

## Phase 3: How to Find Off-Market Deals

### Core approach
1. pull a targeted list
2. skip trace if needed
3. contact owners consistently
4. qualify motivation
5. estimate value fast
6. make simple offers in volume
7. follow up harder than competitors

### Outreach channels
- direct mail
- cold calling
- texting, if compliant in your area and process
- voicemail drops, if compliant
- handwritten letters
- agent outreach
- inbound ads

### What usually creates deals
- timing
- persistence
- solving confusion
- making a simple offer
- following up after the first “not now”

### Seller motivation signs
- tired of paying taxes
- inherited property they do not want
- lives far away
- property is a headache
- problem neighbors or access issues
- needs cash quickly
- had it listed before with no success
- wants easy terms, not top dollar

## Phase 4: Quick Deal Screening
You need to kill bad deals fast.

### Fast-screen checklist
- who owns it?
- what exactly is being sold?
- what is the realistic resale value?
- what is the lowest likely sell-through price?
- what is wrong with it?
- how long might it take to sell?
- is title likely clear?
- is there legal access?
- are taxes current or delinquent?
- are there HOA issues, liens, or restrictions?
- is there buyer demand for this exact asset?

### Fast-screen rule
If you cannot explain in 2 to 3 sentences why someone would buy it from you, do not chase it too long.

## Phase 5: Valuation
Slow flip businesses live or die on valuation.

### Use comparable sales
Focus on:
- same county or submarket
- similar size
- similar road access
- similar utility situation
- similar terrain / usability
- recent solds, not just active listings

### Value anchors
Check:
- sold listings
- current competing inventory
- price per acre or price per square foot of lot
- terms deals versus cash deals
- time on market

### Conservative valuation rule
Base offers on what the property will actually sell for, not what you hope it should sell for.

### Safe approach
- estimate a likely cash resale price
- estimate a likely owner-finance resale price
- use the lower / safer number when offering

## Phase 6: Due Diligence
Before closing, verify the basics.

### Must-check items
- title and vesting
- taxes owed
- liens or judgments
- legal access
- physical access
- flood zone
- wetlands if relevant
- zoning
- HOA / POA restrictions
- setback rules
- utility availability
- survey or boundary issues
- usability of the property
- environmental red flags

### For land specifically
- road frontage
- ingress / egress
- slope / terrain
- buildability
- septic feasibility if rural
- water source options
- parcel shape
- neighboring uses

### For houses specifically
- structure
- roof
- foundation
- HVAC
- plumbing / electrical
- occupancy status
- code issues
- repair estimate range

## Phase 7: Offer Strategy

### Main offer types
- cash offer
- seller finance purchase
- option / control agreement
- subject-to or creative structure where appropriate and legally reviewed
- agent-listed low offer

### Offer formula mindset
Offer based on:
- realistic resale value
- selling costs
- holding costs
- cleanup / improvement costs
- desired profit margin
- risk buffer

### Principles
- make offers simply
- do not over-explain
- anchor with certainty
- leave room for follow-up
- track every offer made

### Example talking point
- “If I pay cash and make it easy, I’d need to be around X.”
- “If price matters most, I may not be your best buyer. If convenience matters, I can probably help.”

## Phase 8: Closing the Deal

### Closing checklist
- signed agreement
- seller identity verified
- title opened
- title issues reviewed
- taxes and liens confirmed
- closing statement reviewed
- exit plan confirmed before funding

### Keep closings simple
Preferred traits:
- reputable title company or closing attorney
- written communication trail
- clear deadlines
- backup plan if issue appears

## Phase 9: How Value Is Created in a Slow Flip
You do not always need major construction.

### Common value-add moves
- buying at the right discount
- cleaning trash / brush
- getting better photos and drone shots
- clarifying access
- confirming zoning or buildability
- resolving title issues
- getting survey / markers
- splitting terms into attractive monthly payments
- better marketing copy
- offering owner financing
- widening buyer pool through multiple listing channels

### Important idea
Many slow flips are won by **information, packaging, and terms**, not heavy rehab.

## Phase 10: Exiting the Deal

### Exit paths
1. resale for cash
2. resale with owner financing
3. wholesale / assign
4. hold for appreciation
5. improve then resell

### Owner-finance advantages
- larger buyer pool
- higher resale price possible
- down payment income
- monthly cash flow
- stronger total ROI on low-basis deals

### Owner-finance considerations
- collection system
- servicing
- repossession / forfeiture laws
- note tracking
- default procedures
- compliance and documentation

## Phase 11: Marketing Your Inventory

### Good listing ingredients
- strong headline
- clear property facts
- map images
- parcel boundaries shown
- best use ideas
- honest downside disclosure
- monthly payment example if financed
- simple call to action

### Places to market
- Facebook Marketplace
- Craigslist
- land websites
- local groups
- email list
- signs where allowed
- investor networks
- agent or flat-fee MLS if it helps

## Phase 15: Risk Management
This matters a lot.

### Biggest risks
- overpaying
- bad valuation
- title defects
- no legal access
- no buyer demand
- underestimating hold time
- regulatory issues
- poor documentation
- weak collections on seller-finance exits

### Risk rules
- buy below market, not at market
- verify, do not assume
- use written checklists
- have multiple exit options
- avoid deals you do not understand
- keep reserves

## Practical Deal-Finding SOP

### Daily / weekly workflow
1. choose 1 market
2. pull a targeted seller list
3. filter for likely motivation
4. contact sellers
5. log every conversation
6. comp promising leads
7. make offers fast
8. follow up repeatedly
9. move accepted deals into due diligence
10. close only after core checks pass
11. relist with strong marketing and terms
12. track outcomes and refine

---

## Simple Rule Set
- buy right
- stay conservative
- move quickly on good leads
- walk away from unclear deals
- follow up longer than others
- create value through clarity and terms
- document everything
- build the machine, not just the next deal
