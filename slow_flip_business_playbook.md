## What a Slow Flip Business Is
A slow flip business usually means:
- buying property at a meaningful discount
- controlling risk by buying right
- improving value through paperwork, access, cleanup, marketing, or terms
- selling for cash or owner financing
- creating spread between acquisition cost and resale value
- repeating the process with systems instead of treating each deal like a one-off hustle

Core idea:
- **You make money when you buy right, structure right, and market right.**

## Phase 1: Define Your Buy Box
Before hunting deals, define exactly what you want.

### Market criteria
- states / counties / zip codes
- urban, suburban, rural, or infill
- population trend
- job growth
- path of development
- days on market
- median price range
- demand from end buyers

### Asset criteria
- vacant land
- houses needing cosmetic work
- mobile-home-friendly lots
- infill lots
- acreage
- rural recreational land
- property with owner-finance buyer demand

### Deal criteria
- target discount percentage
- minimum dollar spread
- minimum monthly cash flow if seller-financed
- max all-in exposure per deal
- clean title only, or title-curable allowed
- access required or not
- utilities required or not

### Simple buy-box example
- vacant land only
- 0.25 to 10 acres
- counties with active land sales
- buy at 25% to 50% of realistic resale value
- prefer direct-to-seller deals
- exit via owner financing whenever possible

## Phase 2: Where to Look for Deals

This first transcript was very clear on one point:
**you do not need to start with expensive marketing.**
The speaker's approach was heavily focused on finding deals already sitting online.

### 1. Facebook Marketplace
This was presented as the top source.

How to use it:
- go to Marketplace
- choose the target city or zip code
- search Home Sales
- set price filters, for example minimum $2,500 and maximum $80,000
- scan listings quickly
- message immediately when a listing is even close
- keep repeating this daily because inventory changes throughout the day

What to look for:
- underpriced houses
- wholesaler listings
- for sale by owner listings
- MLS listings reposted to Facebook
- poor listings with weak photos
- houses that look basically livable

Important lesson:
- many good leads are just sitting in plain sight
- the edge is speed, repetition, and knowing what a good deal looks like

### 2. Craigslist
This was the second favorite source in the transcript.

How to use it:
- search the local real estate for sale section
- use a realistic price filter, for example $1,000 minimum and $50,000 maximum
- scan for investor specials, fixer uppers, FSBOs, and seller-finance listings
- send messages even when the asking price is high, because sellers may take less

What to look for:
- clean houses with ugly listings
- houses with decent interiors and low asking prices
- larger houses that need work but have strong rent potential
- seller-finance opportunities being offered by the current seller

### 3. Zillow / MLS / Redfin
The transcript treated Zillow as another core source, especially for listed deals.

How to use it:
- search a specific zip code or target area
- filter for low price inventory
- look for listings with no photos, stale days on market, and poor agent effort
- contact the listing agent aggressively when price looks workable

What to look for:
- tenant-occupied homes that prove livability
- stale listings with low asking prices
- houses with solid fundamentals but weak presentation
- houses in target areas where a low offer still makes sense

### 4. Other wholesalers
This was another major source from the transcript.

Use wholesalers as deal feeders:
- wholesalers send out houses
- buy the ones that fit your model
- keep the best ones as slow flips
- assign the ones you do not want to keep
- build a network of people who bring you deals and a network of people who buy from you

### 5. Networking as a lead source
A major theme in the transcript was that networking can replace a lot of traditional marketing.

That means:
- build relationships with wholesalers
- build relationships with buyers
- respond fast
- become known as someone who closes
- create a reputation for moving deals quickly

### 6. Driving / virtual neighborhood review
The transcript also used map and street-view thinking.

What to check:
- is the street mostly stable?
- do neighboring houses look occupied?
- is the area mostly intact even if one house is ugly?
- does the neighborhood support rent demand or slow-flip resale demand?

## Phase 3: How to Find Deals Fast Without Big Marketing

### Core approach from transcript 1
1. open Facebook Marketplace
2. search your target zip codes and cities
3. scan listings fast
4. ignore obvious junk
5. click anything that looks remotely workable
6. message sellers immediately
7. make simple offers
8. repeat on Craigslist
9. repeat on Zillow / MLS
10. follow up every couple of days

### Key principle
The transcript's model is not based on heavy outbound marketing.
It is based on:
- online hunting
- speed
- repeated searching
- recognizing value faster than other people
- fast follow-up

### Follow-up behavior from transcript
One example was essentially:
- if seller wants $45,000 and that is too high, send a lower offer anyway
- check back often
- message every couple of days if needed

### Sample simple offer style from transcript
- “I will be around $22,000. I can close by the end of next week. I can cover all your closing costs, so you walk away with a $22,000 check.”

That style works because it is:
- clear
- fast
- easy to understand
- focused on seller outcome

## Phase 4: Quick Deal Screening
You need to kill bad deals fast.

### Main screening lesson from transcript 1
**Cheap does not equal good.**
A $2,000 or $3,000 house may only be a tax bill, not a real opportunity.

### Fast-screen checklist
- who owns it?
- what exactly is being sold?
- what is the asking price?
- what would you actually pay?
- is the property basically livable or is it a total disaster?
- is there obvious fire damage?
- is there a flooded basement?
- are floors buckled?
- is the roof missing?
- are HVAC and water heater still there?
- is the neighborhood stable enough?
- what is realistic rent or resale value?
- does it fit your model, or is it just cheap junk?

### Fast-screen rules pulled from the transcript
- do not buy it just because it is cheap
- if you are mainly buying a tax bill, pass
- if the house has no roof and you can see the sky, usually pass
- if rehab cost will exceed end value, pass
- if everyone has seen the deal and nobody has bought it, be cautious
- if the house is occupied, that is often evidence of livability
- if utilities, HVAC, and water heater are still there, that is a positive signal

### Practice rule
Look at 100 houses before buying 1.
The transcript emphasized repetition so you learn the difference between:
- a good deal
- a cheap bad deal
- a workable slow flip
- a wholesale-only lead

## Phase 5: Valuation
Slow flip businesses live or die on valuation.

### Valuation framework from transcript 1
The speaker kept making rapid judgment calls like:
- what would I actually pay?
- what could I assign it for?
- what could it rent for monthly?
- would this make a solid slow flip?

### Use comparable sales and local familiarity
Focus on:
- same zip code or nearby micro-market
- similar square footage
- similar condition
- similar bed / bath count
- same side of the metro if neighborhood quality changes quickly

### Fast value anchors used in the transcript
- asking price versus likely negotiated price
- realistic rent, often framed as “this could get $700, $800, or $850 a month”
- whether the interior is basically intact
- whether a cleanup is enough versus full rehab
- whether the property is in a known good target area

### Conservative valuation rule
Base offers on what the property will actually sell for or rent for, not what you hope it should do.

### Practical rule
A house can be a deal even at asking price sometimes, but many times the real opportunity is below asking price.
So always separate:
- asking price
- likely accepted price
- ideal buy price
- likely exit price

## Phase 6: Due Diligence
Before closing, verify the basics.

### Must-check items
- title and vesting
- taxes owed
- liens or judgments
- legal access
- physical access
- flood zone
- wetlands if relevant
- zoning
- HOA / POA restrictions
- setback rules
- utility availability
- survey or boundary issues
- usability of the property
- environmental red flags

### For land specifically
- road frontage
- ingress / egress
- slope / terrain
- buildability
- septic feasibility if rural
- water source options
- parcel shape
- neighboring uses

### For houses specifically
- structure
- roof
- foundation
- HVAC
- plumbing / electrical
- occupancy status
- code issues
- repair estimate range

## Phase 7: Offer Strategy

### Main offer types
- cash offer
- seller finance purchase
- option / control agreement
- subject-to or creative structure where appropriate and legally reviewed
- agent-listed low offer

### Offer formula mindset
Offer based on:
- realistic resale value
- selling costs
- holding costs
- cleanup / improvement costs
- desired profit margin
- risk buffer

### Principles
- make offers simply
- do not over-explain
- anchor with certainty
- leave room for follow-up
- track every offer made

### Example talking point
- “If I pay cash and make it easy, I’d need to be around X.”
- “If price matters most, I may not be your best buyer. If convenience matters, I can probably help.”

## Phase 8: Closing the Deal

### Closing checklist
- signed agreement
- seller identity verified
- title opened
- title issues reviewed
- taxes and liens confirmed
- closing statement reviewed
- exit plan confirmed before funding

### Keep closings simple
Preferred traits:
- reputable title company or closing attorney
- written communication trail
- clear deadlines
- backup plan if issue appears

## Phase 9: How Value Is Created in a Slow Flip
You do not always need major construction.

### Common value-add moves from transcript 1
- buying at the right discount
- cleaning out a house full of inherited belongings
- removing trash
- taking better photos
- rewriting a weak listing
- reposting to better channels
- offering owner financing
- assigning to another buyer if it does not fit your hold box
- targeting houses that are rough but still basically usable

### Specific examples from the transcript
Value was often created by:
- finding hidden deals on public platforms
- negotiating below list
- recognizing when a house only needs cleanup, not full rehab
- spotting good rent-to-price ratios
- finding MLS deals ignored by others because agent photos were weak or missing

### Important idea
Many slow flips are won by **recognition, cleanup, packaging, and terms**, not by expensive rehabs.

## Phase 10: Exiting the Deal

### Exit paths
1. resale for cash
2. resale with owner financing
3. wholesale / assign
4. hold for appreciation
5. improve then resell

### Important exit lesson from transcript 1
Not every good lead should be kept.
The speaker repeatedly treated deals as fitting one of three buckets:
- keep as a slow flip
- assign for a quick fee
- pass because it is bad inventory

That decision should happen early.

### Owner-finance advantages
- larger buyer pool
- higher resale price possible
- down payment income
- monthly cash flow
- stronger total ROI on low-basis deals

### Owner-finance considerations
- collection system
- servicing
- repossession / forfeiture laws
- note tracking
- default procedures
- compliance and documentation

## Phase 15: Risk Management
This matters a lot.

### Biggest risks
- overpaying
- bad valuation
- title defects
- no legal access
- no buyer demand
- underestimating hold time
- regulatory issues
- poor documentation
- weak collections on seller-finance exits

### Risk rules
- buy below market, not at market
- verify, do not assume
- use written checklists
- have multiple exit options
- avoid deals you do not understand
- keep reserves

## Practical Deal-Finding SOP

### Daily / weekly workflow
1. choose 1 market
2. open Facebook Marketplace and search your target zip codes
3. scan quickly for anything remotely workable
4. message promising listings immediately
5. repeat on Craigslist
6. repeat on Zillow / MLS
7. log every promising lead
8. comp promising properties fast
9. make simple offers fast
10. follow up repeatedly
11. move accepted deals into due diligence
12. decide early: keep, assign, or pass
13. relist with strong marketing and terms
14. track outcomes and refine
